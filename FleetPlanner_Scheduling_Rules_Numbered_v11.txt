WORKING HOURS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
HR-001 [✓] Driver normal duty hours are planner configurable.
        Set per-driver in the Drivers tab (working_hours_per_day column),
        read by build_driver_profiles().
HR-002 [✓] Maximum duty hours including overtime are planner configurable.
        RESOLVED 2026-08-03. The hidden hardcoded MAX_OVERTIME_HOURS_PER_DAY
        = 2.0 constant is gone. Drivers now have two real, planner-set
        fields (Drivers tab): working_hours_per_day (the normal full day,
        e.g. 9) and max_working_hours_per_day (the hard daily ceiling
        including overtime, e.g. 12). Both are hard rules -- the engine
        will never plan a day under the daily minimum or over the daily
        ceiling for a driver who's used at all that day (see HR-005 for the
        minimum-hours mechanism, which needed a repair pass rather than a
        simple filter). If max_working_hours_per_day is left blank, it
        falls back to working_hours_per_day (zero daily overtime allowed) --
        fail-closed, same precedent as a blank monthly overtime cap,
        deliberately chosen over silently reopening the old
        unlimited-single-day bug.
        Monthly overtime tracking was already solid before this session --
        get_driver_month_overtime_hours() sums finalized-day overtime from
        the 1st of the month correctly. It just wasn't surfaced in the
        Drivers tab; that display gap is still open, see NEW-006 below.
        CRITICAL FIX, 2026-08-03 (same day, after testing against a real
        PLANNED.xlsx): occupied_seconds was a naive SUM of every row's
        duration, including when two rows in the same "Same Driver" group
        are the exact same time slot (e.g. two simultaneous pickups on one
        truck for two different orders -- confirmed as a routine, everyday
        pattern in the real file, not an edge case). This overstated a
        driver's true hours enough to falsely trip the daily ceiling --
        one real driver showed ~17h "occupied" against ~11h of actual
        work. Fixed by switching all hour accounting (daily ceiling,
        minimum, monthly-overtime projection) to the TRUE UNION of a
        driver's time intervals (`_merged_hours()` in
        allocation_engine.py) instead of a running sum -- two overlapping
        rows for one real duty period now count once, exactly like a
        human dispatcher counts them. This was flagged as a known,
        deliberate simplification in the module docstring from an earlier
        session ("if this ever needs to be exact... that is a deliberate
        follow-up") -- this is that follow-up, prompted directly by real
        data proving it was needed, not a guess.
HR-003 [✓] Scheduler must never exceed the configured maximum.
        Verified live: no assignment was produced that exceeded either the
        daily or monthly limits in a direct test.
HR-004 [✓] Idle time counts as break.
        Confirmed: occupied_seconds only accumulates actual job duration,
        never the gap between jobs, so idle time is never charged against a
        driver's hours.
HR-005 [~ WIDENED 2026-08-06] A driver used at all on a given day must
        reach at least their configured working_hours_per_day that day
        (hard minimum, not just a target).
        Added 2026-08-03 at the project owner's explicit request ("full
        rework now to enforce as a hard minimum"). This can't be enforced
        as a simple per-job filter the way the daily ceiling is, because
        the engine assigns jobs one at a time in time order and doesn't
        know a driver's full-day total until the day's last job has been
        considered. It's enforced instead as a repair pass
        (_repair_minimum_daily_hours() in allocation_engine.py) that runs
        after normal allocation: for every driver left with a non-zero,
        under-minimum day, it tries to move ALL of that driver's jobs for
        that day to another qualifying driver with spare room (respecting
        every other hard rule -- license, off-day, shift, overlap, daily
        ceiling, monthly overtime cap). If every job can be moved, the move
        is committed. If even one can't, the whole day is released back to
        unresolved with a clear note ("Below X's minimum daily hours...")
        rather than silently keeping an illegal short day. Runs in a small
        loop (up to 5 passes) since fixing one driver's day can free up
        room that fixes another's.
        Known limitation, disclosed to and accepted by the project owner:
        this is a best-effort greedy heuristic, not a global optimizer --
        with limited test data available, it hasn't been validated against
        a real full day's volume yet (see TESTING NOTES). It also
        deliberately does not touch jobs inside a "Same Driver" group
        (planner-flagged pairings are left alone), and does not attempt a
        supplier fallback for a released day (flagged for manual review
        instead, since moving cost to a supplier is a planner decision).
        VALIDATED AND REFINED against real data, 2026-08-03 (same day):
        reconstructing a real PLANNED.xlsx (stripping the human's driver/
        vehicle assignments and re-running allocate() from scratch) showed
        several real drivers legitimately end their day under 9h -- e.g.
        one driver's whole day was two simultaneous pickups for one 20h
        order (5h true work after the hour-accounting fix above), nothing
        else. In every such case the shortfall was entirely inside a
        "Same Driver" flagged group. The original scope note above ("does
        not touch grouped jobs") was correct but the total-hours
        CALCULATION had a gap: it only summed the driver's UNGROUPED jobs,
        silently ignoring the grouped ones' hours entirely when deciding
        whether the day was under minimum. Fixed: total_hours is now the
        true merged total across EVERY job the driver has that day
        (grouped and ungrouped); if any of those jobs are grouped, the day
        is now correctly recognized as unfixable-without-touching-a-
        protected-group and left alone entirely, rather than incorrectly
        moving/releasing the ungrouped remainder (which could never have
        fixed the shortfall anyway, since the group's own hours are what's
        driving it). A second, independent staleness/tentative-tracking
        bug was also caught and fixed in this same function -- see
        CHANGELOG_AI.md Phase 12 for the full detail.
        WIDENED 2026-08-06 (see the v10 addendum below for full detail):
        the "skip any grouped day entirely" scope above, while correct in
        principle, turned out to defeat this whole repair pass in
        practice on a real day where 84% of rows were grouped -- the
        pass almost never ran at all, and severe imbalance (2h vs 16h
        drivers on the same day) went completely unaddressed. Confirmed
        with the project owner and fixed: a grouped day can now be moved
        WHOLE onto one other qualifying driver (never split -- the "same
        driver" instruction is still honored), with SD-004 vehicle-type
        consistency enforced on the new driver via a new helper,
        `_established_group_vehicle_type()`. If no single driver can
        legally take the group, it's still released to unresolved exactly
        as before -- a real, expected outcome sometimes, not a bug. Status
        marked [~] rather than [✓] because real-data testing (see the v10
        addendum) shows this still doesn't reach full parity with a human
        planner's balance -- a few drivers can still land under minimum
        or fully idle, as an artifact of the greedy processing order, not
        a further known bug.

SHIFT (NEW section -- added 2026-08-03)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Background: the field previously called "Shift start" was a free-typed
exact clock time (e.g. "07:00 AM"), set by the planner BEFORE planning,
and enforced as an absolute wall -- no job before that exact minute could
ever be assigned to that driver. The project owner flagged this as
backwards: in real operation, the planner never fixes an exact time in
advance -- they just decide, per driver, whether that driver is running
mornings or evenings, and the driver's actual first-job time is only
known -- and announced to the driver -- after the plan is built. A rigid
15-days-morning/15-days-evening rotation with automatic transition rules
was discussed and explicitly rejected in favor of a simpler model (see
SS-002).

SS-001 [✓ NEW] Shift start is never fixed by the planner before planning.
        The planner sets a simple label per driver -- "Morning", "Evening",
        or no restriction -- not a clock time. The engine enforces this as
        a window (morning = before 12:00, evening = 12:00 onward; see
        SHIFT_PERIOD_EVENING_CUTOFF_HOUR in allocation_engine.py if this
        cutoff ever needs to change). The driver's exact first-job time for
        a given day comes out of whichever job the plan happens to give
        them first -- it is never chosen in advance.
SS-002 [✓ NEW] No automatic 15-day rotation or off-day-triggered transition
        logic.
        Explicitly decided against, at the project owner's request: "if the
        planner is deciding which driver to bring in morning and evening we
        dont have to track this." The planner can change a driver's
        Morning/Evening label whenever they choose -- the software does not
        compute, enforce, or remember any rotation schedule, and does not
        restrict when a driver can be switched between the two.
SS-003 [NEW, open follow-up] Reporting each driver's actual first-job time
        back to them after planning.
        The data needed for this already exists in a finalized day's plan
        (the earliest assigned job's start time per driver per day) but
        isn't currently surfaced anywhere in export.py or digest_generator.py.
        Not built this session -- flagged as a natural next step if the
        planner wants an actual "your shift starts at X today" line in the
        driver-facing output.

DRIVER RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
DR-001 [✓] Drivers may change vehicles during the day.
        Vehicle choice is re-evaluated per job; nothing locks a driver to one
        vehicle plate for the day (outside of the "Same Driver" vehicle
        preference, which is a soft preference, not a lock).
DR-002 [✓] Driver cannot have overlapping assigned jobs in automatic scheduling.
        Verified live against the full UNPLANNED.xlsx run: 0 overlap
        violations across all assigned drivers.
DR-003 [X] Jobs ending exactly when another starts are not overlapping.
        Verified NOT working with a direct test: one driver, one vehicle,
        job A 08:00-10:00 and job B 10:00-12:00 (back-to-back, no "Same
        Driver" flag). Job B was rejected as "No qualifying in-house or
        supplier resource available" even though the same driver/vehicle
        were free. Root cause below (DR-004).
DR-004 [X] Travel time is ignored because planner already included it.
        Contradicted by the code: allocation_engine.py applies a hardcoded
        DEFAULT_TRAVEL_BUFFER_MINUTES = 30 on every overlap check, for every
        job, everywhere in the engine. This buffer is never overridden from
        the UI or settings -- it is always 30 minutes. This is what causes
        the DR-003 failure above, and will also silently swallow any two
        back-to-back or near-back-to-back jobs for the same driver/vehicle
        that aren't explicitly tied together with a "Same Driver" tag,
        forcing them to an (unnecessary) supplier hire or leaving them
        unresolved. Recommend either removing the buffer entirely (per
        DR-004 as written) or making it a planner-configurable setting
        defaulting to 0.
        NOTE: DR-003/DR-004 read as still-open findings in this legend
        entry, but see TB-001 below -- this was in fact resolved
        2026-08-03 (buffer changed to 0). Left as-is here rather than
        rewritten, matching this document's existing convention of not
        editing older numbered entries' verified-status text after the
        fact except through their own addendum; TB-001 is the entry of
        record for the resolution.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
VEHICLE RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
VR-001 [✓] Vehicle may have multiple drivers during the day.
        Vehicles are not tied to a single driver; build_vehicle_profiles()
        tracks only busy_intervals, not an owning driver.
VR-002 [✓] Vehicle cannot be double-booked unless planner grouping allows it.
        Same overlap + ignore_group_key mechanism as drivers, and it applies
        to vehicle busy_intervals too, so a "Same Driver" flagged group can
        legitimately double up on a vehicle by design; anything outside a
        group is a hard block. (Same 30-min buffer caveat as DR-004 applies
        here as well for un-flagged jobs -- see TB-001, resolved.)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
SAME DRIVER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
SD-001 [✓] Same Driver is a planning hint.
        Implemented as same_driver_key from the "Same Driver" column --
        explicitly documented as planner-flagged, not AI-driven.
SD-002 [✓] Keep same driver when feasible.
        group_candidates logic prefers a driver already in the flagged group
        before falling back to the normal least-occupied-driver ranking.
SD-003 [✓] Split only when required by hard rules.
        A new driver is only pulled into a group when none of the current
        group's drivers still qualify (hours, license, or overlap) for the
        next row -- confirmed in code comments and logic.
SD-004 [✓ RESOLVED 2026-08-03] A driver cannot be on two different
        vehicles at the same time, even within one "Same Driver" flagged
        group.
        CONFIRMED REAL BUG via a real software-output test file: one
        driver was assigned a 10 Ton Chiller Truck 23:00-00:00 AND a 20
        Seater Bus 23:00-01:00 SIMULTANEOUSLY, both rows sharing the same
        flagged group. Root cause: the overlap-relaxation that lets a
        "Same Driver" group's rows overlap in time (needed for the
        legitimate case -- one truck doing two simultaneous pickups for
        two different orders) was keyed PURELY on the group tag matching,
        with no check on whether it was actually the same vehicle/type
        involved. It fired for ANY two rows sharing a group tag,
        including ones needing genuinely different vehicles.
        FIXED: the relaxation now also requires that the driver's
        already-established vehicle for that group (tracked via
        group_vehicle_by_driver) is the same TYPE as what the new row
        needs. If a driver's group vehicle is a Chiller Truck and the
        next row in that group needs a Bus, the overlap check runs
        normally for that driver -- if they're already busy at that time
        (as in the real bug), they're correctly excluded, and the group
        naturally splits into two consistent driver-vehicle threads
        instead (matching the real ground-truth pattern already seen in
        earlier PLANNED.xlsx analysis -- e.g. one driver handles all of a
        group's Chiller rows, another handles all its Bus rows). A driver
        picking up a different vehicle type within the same group at a
        NON-overlapping time is still correctly allowed (confirmed
        against real ground-truth behavior where this happens routinely).
        See tests/test_same_driver_vehicle_consistency.py.
        EXTENDED 2026-08-06: the same SD-004 vehicle-type-consistency
        check is now also enforced when HR-005's repair pass moves a
        whole grouped day onto a NEW driver (see the widened HR-005 entry
        and the v10 addendum) via a new helper,
        `_established_group_vehicle_type()`. A group can never end up
        split across two vehicle types on one driver, whether it's being
        assigned for the first time or being relocated by the repair
        pass afterward.
SD-005 [NEW, 2026-08-06] A group's opening driver is chosen by projecting
        the WHOLE group's hours, not just its first row.
        Previously (see SD-002/SD-003) a group's first row picked its
        driver purely by who had the fewest occupied hours AT THAT
        INSTANT -- with no idea whether the group about to land on them
        was a 2h errand or an 11h event. A driver who happened to be idle
        when a large group started could end up carrying that whole group
        alone while everyone else stayed comparatively empty, with no way
        to reconsider once picked (SD-002/SD-003 lock a group to its
        first qualifying driver from then on). Confirmed as a real driver
        of the imbalance the project owner reported (see the v10
        addendum). FIXED: each group's total merged hours are precomputed
        once before the main allocation loop (via the existing
        `_merged_hours()`), and a fresh group's opening row now picks the
        candidate that minimizes `occupied_seconds + projected group
        hours`, not just `occupied_seconds` alone. A group's second and
        later rows are unaffected -- they still prefer the group's
        already-established driver first, per SD-002/SD-003.
        NOTE (2026-08-06, same day): this SD-005 fix applies only to
        allocate() -- the two experimental strategies below (see
        EXPERIMENTAL ALLOCATION STRATEGIES) handle group-leader selection
        differently (allocate_by_merit via event-diverse seeding,
        allocate_by_anchor via most-constrained-driver-first anchoring),
        not via this projected-hours mechanism.

SD-006 [NEW, 2026-08-06] "Every driver has real work" is a first-class
        goal, not just "no driver is illegally under-minimum."
        Confirmed by directly comparing real automated output to the
        real human-planned PLANNED.xlsx: the human plan uses all 11
        drivers, none below 7.5h, while both allocate() (with the v10
        fix) and allocate_by_merit() were leaving 2 drivers at a legal
        but unrealistic 0h -- almost always a SIDE EFFECT of HR-005's
        repair pass freeing a short day entirely to fix someone else's
        minimum, with nothing afterward ever revisiting that now-idle
        driver. FIXED: new pass, `_rebalance_idle_drivers()`, wired into
        the rearrangement loop of allocate(), allocate_by_merit(), AND
        allocate_by_anchor() alike. For every 0h driver who's otherwise
        available: tries to accumulate enough real work to reach THEIR
        OWN minimum, first from anything still unresolved (free), then
        from genuine SURPLUS on other drivers (hours above their own
        minimum, never dropping a donor below it). Nothing is committed
        to real state until the FULL accumulated total clears the idle
        driver's minimum -- if it can't be reached, everything tentative
        is simply discarded, leaving the driver correctly at a legal 0h
        rather than trading one violation for another.
        Two real bugs were found and fixed building this, both caught by
        the EXISTING test suite (tests/test_daily_overtime_ceiling.py)
        before either reached the project owner: (1) a genuinely
        unfixable released job got rescued right back onto the exact
        driver it was correctly released from, because the first version
        committed the first feasible candidate without checking the FULL
        total against the minimum; (2) a donor's "remaining hours after
        removal" calculation excluded their already-settled jobs from the
        baseline workload entirely, making them look like they'd drop to
        a legal 0h when they'd actually be left with an illegal short
        day. See CHANGELOG_AI.md Phase 15 for the full write-up.
        Scoped to ungrouped jobs only in this first version -- pulling a
        whole "Same Driver" group away from a donor to rescue an idle
        driver is a further step, not attempted yet.

EXPERIMENTAL ALLOCATION STRATEGIES (NEW section -- added 2026-08-06)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Background: right after the v10 fix, the project owner raised two
structural concerns about allocate() itself: "Same Driver" grouping felt
like it was ruling the allocation rather than assisting it (though
confirmed genuinely load-bearing -- removing it entirely from a real day
made results WORSE, 12 unresolved), and driver selection was effectively
alphabetical in several places rather than merit-based. Rather than patch
allocate() further, two full alternative strategies were built alongside
it (never replacing it -- allocate() is unchanged by this section, and
plan_day_tab.py still calls only allocate()).

EXP-001 [~ NEW] allocate_by_merit(): shift-partitioned, event-diverse
        seeding.
        Design: (1) pairs-only Same-Driver pre-merge into PlanningUnit
        objects (never 3+ rows merged into one) -- turns the genuinely-
        simultaneous case into a single decision instead of allocate()'s
        runtime overlap-relaxation machinery, while export still shows
        every original row separately; (2) partition into morning/evening
        driver and job pools; (3) SEED each driver's first job
        diversified by EVENT, not raw job order -- prefer a new event
        over a second job from one someone else already started
        (confirmed valuable by the project owner: keeps an event's
        footprint on as few drivers as possible, easier to hand to a
        supplier later); license scarcity ALWAYS overrides seeding, the
        only qualified driver takes a job immediately if free; (4) FILL
        everything else, least-occupied-first.
        Real-data result (UNPLANNED.xlsx + fleetplanner.db): 16
        unresolved vs. allocate()'s 12 -- WORSE. The aggressive
        event-diverse seeding spends driver availability faster than the
        rearrangement stage can recover it. Disclosed as underperforming,
        kept in the codebase as a tested alternative, not recommended for
        use as-is.
EXP-002 [~ NEW] allocate_by_anchor(): anchor-first-and-last-job,
        most-constrained drivers first.
        Design, from the project owner's own idea: instead of hoping a
        good day shape falls out of one-job-at-a-time decisions, size
        each driver's day intentionally. For each driver, narrowest
        license first (specialists claim their anchors before generalists
        absorb whatever's left): give them their earliest-available
        qualifying job as the FIRST anchor, compute their target finish
        time (first job's end + their ceiling), and search for whichever
        remaining job ends closest to -- but not after -- that target as
        the LAST anchor. Confirmed with the project owner:
        max_working_hours_per_day left blank means the ceiling EQUALS
        working_hours_per_day itself (a fixed day, not a range) --
        confirmed against the real PLANNED.xlsx, where the only two
        drivers landing at exactly 9h are precisely the two with no max
        configured. Everything else in the middle goes to whoever it
        fits, least-occupied-first. Then a bounded SWAP REPAIR (capped at
        3 rounds by default): for each still-unresolved unit, look for a
        driver who could take it if exactly ONE of their existing
        single-job, ungrouped assignments moved elsewhere, and only
        commit the swap if that displaced job actually finds a legal new
        home -- a strict, verified improvement, never a net-zero
        shuffle.
        A real bug was found and fixed before this reached real testing:
        the supplier-fallback branches set assigned_supplier_unit but
        forgot to reset job.unresolved back to False (a leftover from
        provisionally marking every pre-swap leftover unit unresolved) --
        successfully-supplied jobs were silently still counted as
        unresolved. Fixed by adding the missing reset in both commit
        paths.
        Real-data result: 12 unresolved, tied with allocate()'s baseline,
        but with a real, verified improvement in driver utilization --
        9/9 active drivers had real work (2h-12h) vs. allocate()'s 9/11
        with 2 fully idle. Confirmed the anchor-first design is
        directionally sound even though it hasn't beaten the baseline on
        raw unresolved count yet.
EXP-003 [OPEN, not decided] Which strategy, if any, should
        plan_day_tab.py eventually use?
        Not decided this session, deliberately (Rule 16). Neither new
        strategy has dedicated synthetic tests yet -- both were validated

OPT-001 [~] Calculate each driver's duty span.
        STILL not a distinct "duty span" object -- see 2026-08-03 update
        below. Every hard rule (daily ceiling, daily minimum, monthly
        overtime) still checks summed job DURATION (occupied_seconds), not
        elapsed span (first job start to last job end). Confirmed as a
        real symptom by the project owner testing a live UNPLANNED.xlsx
        run: a driver got a 13:00-15:00 job and a 22:00-01:00 job (5h of
        actual work) across a 12h span with a 7h idle hole in the middle,
        and none of the hour-based hard rules had any way to see that.
        OPEN QUESTION, discussed 2026-08-03 but not yet decided: should a
        driver's SPAN count toward daily hour limits instead of (or in
        addition to) summed duration? Tradeoff explained to the project
        owner: span-based is more realistic about a driver being
        unavailable for other work during a gap, but would hit daily/
        monthly ceilings much faster than actual hours worked and may not
        match how drivers are paid. Not resolved -- flagged in
        NEXT_SESSION.md for a future session once the gap-filling fix
        below has had a chance to reduce how often this scenario even
        comes up.
OPT-002 [✓ PARTIAL, 2026-08-03] Detect idle gaps.
        `_driver_has_bounded_gap_fit()` (allocation_engine.py) now exists
        and can identify a genuine gap in a driver's day: a job slot that
        sits strictly between an already-assigned job before it and one
        after it (with the normal travel buffer on both sides). It is
        used by OPT-003 below. It is NOT used to detect gaps proactively
        for reporting/display purposes -- only as an internal check
        during the fix below.
OPT-003 [✓ PARTIAL, 2026-08-03] Automatically insert jobs that legally fit
        gaps.
        Fixed for the confirmed real-world case: `allocation_engine.
        _fill_gaps_with_unresolved_jobs()` runs once after the main
        greedy pass (and after suppliers), and for every job still fully
        unresolved, checks whether some driver now has a bounded gap
        (per OPT-002) that the job fits into -- respecting every other
        hard rule (license, off-day, shift, overlap, daily ceiling,
        monthly overtime) -- and assigns it there instead of leaving it
        unresolved. This CANNOT be done inside the main greedy pass
        itself: jobs are processed strictly in start-time order, so the
        LATER of a gap's two bounding jobs is never assigned yet when an
        earlier-starting job that would sit inside that gap is being
        considered -- confirmed by testing an in-pass version first and
        finding it structurally could not fire (see CHANGELOG_AI.md
        Phase 11 for the detail). A real bug was also found and fixed in
        the neighboring HR-005 minimum-hours repair pass while building
        this: it was working off a one-time snapshot of each driver's job
        list, so a fix made for one driver during a pass could leave a
        LATER driver's fix in the same pass reasoning about stale data --
        jobs visibly ping-ponged between two drivers instead of settling.
        Fixed by recomputing each driver's actual current day fresh right
        before processing it, and by tracking hours/overlaps tentatively
        added within a single move-batch so two moves to the same new
        driver can't each look individually legal but jointly exceed a
        hard rule. See tests/test_gap_filling.py.
        Deliberately NOT done: reclaiming a job already given to a
        SUPPLIER back to an in-house driver's newly-available gap --
        unwinding a SupplierHire safely (renumbering, freeing capacity)
        is separate, riskier work not attempted this session. Only jobs
        that are still fully unresolved (no driver AND no supplier) are
        considered.
        RELATED, 2026-08-06: a second, structurally similar ping-pong bug
        was found in this same HR-005 repair pass after it was widened to
        move whole "Same Driver" groups -- see the widened HR-005 entry
        and the v10 addendum below. Fixed with a `settled_job_ids`
        stability guard, the same general category of fix as the one
        described here, applied to the newly-widened move logic.
OPT-004 [~] If no legal fit exists, generate Planner Suggestions only.
        Partially true: when no legal in-house/supplier fit exists, the job
        is marked unresolved with a note (confirmed in the live test -- 10
        rows). But nothing resembling a "Planner Suggestion" object is
        generated for it -- it's simply left blank/unresolved.
OPT-005 [X] Planner Suggestions may include small overlaps for planner review only.
        Not implemented. There is no mechanism anywhere that proposes an
        overlapping assignment for review. An unresolved job stays fully
        unassigned; the engine never suggests a "tight but possible" overlap.
OPT-006 [X] Suggested overlaps are never auto-approved.

OPT-007 [~] Highlight Planner Suggestions visually.

NEW-007 [~ PARTIALLY RESOLVED 2026-08-03, same day] Underutilized drivers
        while other jobs sit unresolved -- two distinct causes found.
        Originally framed as: the OPT-002/003 gap-filler only covers a
        driver with a GENUINE BOUNDED gap (an existing booking before AND
        after); it does nothing for a driver who is simply underutilized
        with wide-open capacity on one or both sides. Observed in a real
        software-output test: one driver ended the day with a single 2h
        job and nothing else, while several jobs sat unresolved. Checked
        whether those specific unresolved jobs matched that driver's
        likely vehicle-type license -- they didn't appear to, so that
        specific instance looked like a correct license-mismatch outcome,
        not a bug.
        SHARPER DIAGNOSIS from the project owner, same day: proposed that
        the engine should recognize vehicle-type QUALIFICATION SCARCITY --
        a driver licensed for ONLY one vehicle type is a non-substitutable
        resource for that type, and their hours should be reserved for it
        rather than spent on a job a more broadly-licensed driver could
        equally cover. Concrete real example given: a driver licensed
        ONLY for "10 Ton Chiller Truck" had that day's full set of Chiller
        requests (12:00-17:00 x2 simultaneous, 17:00-22:00, 23:00-00:00
        x2 simultaneous -- 11h true/merged work) fit cleanly inside their
        hour rule -- but if an earlier, non-exclusive Chiller job (one a
        broadly-licensed driver could also cover) had landed on them
        first, it could have burned just enough capacity to push one of
        the later exclusive requests over their ceiling, to
        supplier/unresolved, even with an idle qualifying driver
        available.
        FIXED (specialist-reservation ranking): for an UNGROUPED job with
        multiple qualifying candidates, the engine now prefers the more
        broadly-licensed ("generalist") candidate over a narrowly-licensed
        ("specialist") one, reserving the specialist's hours for
        exclusive-type demand. Ranking key: `(-len(driver.license_types),
        driver.occupied_seconds)` -- license breadth first, existing
        hours-fairness as the tiebreak. Since the daily/monthly hard-rule
        ceiling checks still run BEFORE this ranking (filtering candidates
        first), a generalist can never be pushed over their own limit by
        this preference -- they simply get preferred up to their own
        ceiling, then naturally drop out of candidacy and whoever's next
        (another generalist, or the specialist) takes over.
        DELIBERATELY SCOPED OUT: "Same Driver" grouped jobs, including a
        group's very first assignment. Tried applying the same ranking
        there first and it backfired -- since neither driver is yet in
        group_drivers[group_key] for a group's opening row, both fall into
        the same general candidate pool this ranking touches, and it can
        steal a group's first job away from a specialist toward a
        generalist, fragmenting a block of work that should have started
        and stayed on one driver. Group continuity (the existing
        group_candidates preference, and -- as of 2026-08-06, see SD-005 --
        projected-group-hours fairness for a group's first assignment)
        already handles consolidation correctly on its own and takes
        priority; the specialist-reservation heuristic only applies once
        there's no group to defer to.
        SIDE FINDING, not addressed: while building the regression test,
        surfaced a real interaction between this fix and HR-005 (the daily
        minimum-hours rule) worth knowing about -- if a generalist is
        given just ONE small ungrouped job (e.g. 2h) via this new
        preference and nothing else fills their day, HR-005's repair pass
        will try to move that 2h job elsewhere for being under-minimum;
        if no one else has room, it releases to unresolved rather than
        just leaving the generalist with a short, perfectly legal-in-

        UPDATE 2026-08-06: this same side finding is now also visible on

NEW-010 [NEW, 2026-08-06] Fixing real data issues can increase the

Addendum, 2026-08-06 (v10 -- HR-005 widened, SD-005 look-ahead group-leader
selection, two real thrashing/ordering bugs found and fixed, NEW-008):
- Triggered by the project owner directly cloning the real repo state and
  reporting that hour balance still didn't match the human-planned
  reference file, despite SD-004 and TB-001 already being fixed -- some
  drivers landed at 12h, others at 2h, on the same real day.
- ROOT CAUSE, confirmed by running the real allocate() against the real
  UNPLANNED.xlsx + fleetplanner.db (not guessed): 37 of 44 rows (84%) on
  this real day carried a non-blank "Same Driver" value -- essentially one
  group per event. HR-005's repair pass unconditionally skipped ANY
  driver-day containing a grouped job (correct in principle per the
  2026-08-03 refinement -- some shortfalls genuinely can't be fixed
  without touching a protected group). But with the large majority of
  rows grouped, that skip meant the daily-minimum safety net almost never
  ran at all on this kind of day -- whichever driver a group happened to
  land on (via the old "least occupied AT THIS INSTANT" pick for the
  group's opening row) kept that group's hours regardless of the
  resulting imbalance, with nothing downstream able to touch it.
- TWO CHANGES approved together by the project owner (asked explicitly
  rather than assumed, per Rule 16): SD-005 (look-ahead group-leader
  selection, projecting a fresh group's TOTAL merged hours onto each
  candidate instead of just its opening row) and the HR-005 widening
  (grouped days can now be moved WHOLE by the repair pass, with SD-004
  vehicle-type consistency enforced on the receiving driver via a new
  `_established_group_vehicle_type()` helper).
- A REAL THRASHING BUG was found and fixed while building the HR-005
  widening, before it ever reached the project owner: once whole groups
  became movable, a short group moved onto a driver with room could make
  THAT driver's own day look under-minimum to the very next repair pass,
  triggering a DIFFERENT group to be moved onto them next, and so on --
  groups visibly bounced between drivers across the 5-pass loop instead
  of settling (caught by tracing driver state pass-by-pass against the
  real file, not a synthetic guess). Fixed with a `settled_job_ids`
  stability guard threaded through all 5 passes of one allocate() call:
  once a job has been moved by the repair pass, it is never moved again
  for the rest of that run -- trading a small amount of theoretical
  optimality for a guaranteed, deterministic stop, consistent with this
  function's existing "best-effort heuristic" framing.
- A SECOND, smaller bug found the same way: the repair pass's search for
  a new driver picked the FIRST feasible match in driver_pool's natural
  (alphabetical) order, not the most-free one. On the real data this
  meant a driver who simply hadn't had their own under-minimum day
  resolved YET in the same pass could look "busy" (still holding their
  original jobs) and get skipped in favor of a driver who happened to
  have just been freed moments earlier by an unrelated fix, purely due to
  processing order. Fixed by searching candidates in
  least-occupied-hours-first order instead of list order.
- REAL-DATA RESULT (same UNPLANNED.xlsx + fleetplanner.db used in the
  2026-08-03 addendum above): driver-hour spread went from a 2.0h-16.0h
  summed range (11.0h merged max) before this fix to a 4.0h-11.0h merged
  range after, with most drivers landing exactly at their 9h minimum.
  The real human-planned PLANNED.xlsx reference spread was 5.0h-12.0h
  across 11 drivers for comparison. NOT yet full parity: a few drivers
  still land under 9h and two ended up fully idle in this specific run --
  disclosed honestly as a processing-order limitation of the greedy
  heuristic (see SE-003, HR-005), not a further bug to chase blindly.
- Full existing test suite (test_daily_overtime_ceiling.py,
  test_gap_filling.py, test_hour_accounting.py, test_license_and_hours.py,

  test_same_driver_vehicle_consistency.py, test_shift_period.py,

  (test_shift_start.py remains a pre-existing dead leftover from before
